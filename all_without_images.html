"Well, of course not. If you borrow a trillion dollars to build a 1¢ aspirin machine to compete with $1 generic aspirin, you are strongly incentivized to sell your aspirin for 99¢. "I’m surprised I haven’t see anyone say this yet, but I don’t let the LLM design the code at all, I let it implement a design I have created.By that I mean that, rather than let the LLM do the entire structure, I take the time to create the full hierarchy of abstractions and actors that I want my code to do. I may not know how each unit is implemented, but I know each unit’s responsibilities and how they interact with one another. I’ve found that LLMs are HORRIBLE at writing decent abstractions, regardless of how much prompting and skills I’ve given it. It will always get the job done, but it will often be unmaintainable and require bigger and heavier refactors as I introduce more and more requirement changes.But by flexing my basic design skills, I don’t have those issues. Future refactoring fit into easy abstractions that I’ve designed. And by documenting it up front, I also have what becomes a living document on how this work will turn out.It takes a few hours up front to plan ahead, but it makes the LLMs job easier and use less tokens, and it makes the code better long term.
