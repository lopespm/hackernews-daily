H.264 Is Magic (2016)

"Had the pleasure of working with Jason Garrett-Glaser (better known as DarkShikari) circa 2011 on a patch for x264 to better utilize more cores (slice-threaded lookahead). At the time he was working with Sony to optimize their PlayStation game streaming service, and this had the potential to lower encoding latency (critical to game streaming) even on same-core systems by spreading the frame over more cores to do more parallel work, and doing more intelligent scheduling and convergence after that tile-based work was reconverged into a frame. At the time we were using servers with 48 physical cores to do transcoding, a bit of a rarity in a single NUMA space back then and there were no instances to test this on so I flamegraph traced a few encodes at very high thread parallel counts and send back the instrumented profiles (from a custom compiled version) showing the core work being done and he used that to optimize the motion estimation code which worked on the first shot to perfectly level out cpu usage and increase encoding speed across ~24 cores (was previously topping out around 8 and running one core hot iirc). I got a look into how exotic and complex this project was, particularly in terms how low level and advanced things like motion estimation, quantizing and perceptual compression were... it was and still is sort of magic how many smart techniques are baked into something that we barely pay attention to. They were, even at that time, working on x265.His blog, Diary of an x264 developer, was a great read too for anyone wanting to get a vantage point on how crazy that codebase was.[1]https://github.com/mstorsjo/x264/blob/master/doc/threads.txt(edit: was Gaikai that was acquired by Sony in 2012 and is the basis for PlayStation Now)"

"Ten years later, 2026 magic: AV1https://engineering.fb.com/2026/06/22/video-engineering/adop...And soon AV2https://av2.aomedia.org/"

"One uncommonly used feature of H.264 is support for lossless video. It's a bit hard to use due to tooling (not easy to discover and select the feature), and is sometimes incompatible with web browsers, but you can get actual lossless video using a mainstream video codec. You can verify that it really is lossless, and doesn't have any conversion step where it's quantized to 8-bit YUV."x264vfw" is one tool which supports this format, even inside of an AVI file. So older software that uses Video For Windows can create those files."
