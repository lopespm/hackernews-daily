LLM agents work for their parent companies, not for you

"A LLM should be considered an advisor to the same extent as legal, financial, and medical. They should be legally required to act to the best of their capability in the interests of their client, subject to the same requirements that they don't break the law to do so as applies to legal, financial, or medical advisors.An important part of that is that they must never represent themselves as acting for someone who is not their client.This still allows them to work for you while being the client of their parent companies, but it would disallow claiming to be serving a client when that person is not their client.When an AI says it is serving your best interests, it must actually be doing so.The nature of client confidentiality is that it exists because in order to advise appropriately, a full understanding of the client's situation might be necessary. The legal protection of confidentiality enables disclosing the required information without fear of making it public. That too should be protected with AI in your service.All of this is established principle. I don't see any reason why this should not be applied to AI as well. The same principles have the same reasoning as the current legally protected advisors.This is an absolute must for the common benefit to society. The alternative is a world where people increasingly trust the ever-more-reasonable sounding AIs while the answer to the question "Who should I vote for?" is available to the highest bidder."

"I find the argument here pretty flimsy. The author is upset that Claude respects Bandcamp's wish to not make its site accessible to LLMs? Clearly the alternative is worse. If LLMs ran around disregarding the rules because they held their users' desires above all else, surely they would be met with extreme consequences that would limit their utility to those same users in the long term.It seems the outcome in the post is more a result of the usage policies of the sites in question, rather than the fault of the LLM or its creator.I'm not claiming these AI companies are somehow incapable of wrongdoing. We've already seen a lot of dubious behavior and I'm sure we'll see a lot more. But I do think these companies owe it to their investors and to society to try to put reasonable guardrails around how their products are used. We shouldn't fault them for doing so."

"Might be a bit of a controversial take but I think these types of refusals from the closed models are correct. You're attempting to break the ToS of Bandcamp, that's pretty cut and dry. They do not want agents crawling over their site. Imagine how much spammier and scammier the internet would be if it were a free for all and everyone's agents obeyed these requests.I went through this the other day, I wanted Claude to scrape some 3D models from McMaster, and it refused because it was against the rules of the site. I had to go through the moderately painful process of getting approved for their API instead.Of course, you can get an abliterated model to do this for you, but I'm glad there's some amount of friction there preventing 99% of people from doing stuff like this."
