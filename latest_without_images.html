UK government forced to admit it issued Apple a secret encryption order

"Apple has significantly weakened its security assurances in a way that is arguably deceptive. They used to say nobody can access your data, not even Apple. Now they say Apple cannot access your data. People will point out that your key is created on your device, lives on your device, and never leaves your device, but that is just your key. Your key is likely a KEK (key encryption key) that opens another key (not “your” key, let’s call it the actual key) that your data is secured with. There can be other parties (UK government) with their own KEK that opens a copy of the actual key. In this scenario, Apple can have no access to a KEK that opens the actual key, but they are forced to make the wording change they did, if they want to stay honest.I say the statement is arguably deceptive because it presents the same feel of assurance that the old one did, to most people.Note John Ternus in a recent event (WWDC keynote if I recall correctly) used the old wording.
