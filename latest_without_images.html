Bleached corals thrive within days when touching healthy neighbour

"Coral gets its color from algae called zooxanthellae, which it depends on for photosynthesis.Bleaching is actually due to the coral expelling those algae. Bleached corals are not dead, and remarkably they can not only let the algae in again but can also select a different algae species to cultivate (changing colour in the process)!.This is thought to be a deliberate survival strategy to optimize the species of algae under different conditions (Adaptive Bleaching Hypothesis).Bleaching is caused by the same stressors (like high temperature) which kill coral, but bleaching itself is not death of the coral."

"Having been a diver on the Great Barrier Reef numerous times, the scale of bleaching events means that there is often no non-bleached neighbour for vast distances. Healthy propagation rarely occurs before the brown sludge sets in, then its game over."

I love software rendering

"Software rendering often provided better visual effects than the GPU paths could provide at the time. A general purpose CPU can technically do any effect since the software is the pixel shader.Programmable shader pipelines weren't a thing until Geforce 3 in 2001."

"This isn't software rendering, it's called turning off spatial anti-aliasing. You can still do that in the modernest of modern games. Software rendering went out of the window because the CPU tends to have other things to do whilst playing the game besides only multiplying 4x4 matrices, which was shifted to the shader cores of GPUs. The look-and-feel that the author is discussing including shadow and texture LUTs can also be done on GPUs by passing the tables in to shaders as constant read-only storage buffers; that is not specific to software rendering. It bears mentioning that many modern software renderers (LLVMpipe/Lavapipe, OpenSWR, WARP) are basically full D3D/OpenGL/Vulkan devices in their own right, including shader compilers.Also, modern spatial anti-aliasing works by multisampling within a pixel (or even neighbouring pixels) and taking the average, rather than filtering across singly-sampled pixels, which massively reduces the quality. Not to mention temporal and machine learning AA techniques including DLSS, XeSS, FSR, and all the TAA implementations in many games."

"Ah the software rendering era. Where I gained 10fps by adding a sound card."
