Microsoft blames monster email outage on software defect introduced in July fix

"No big surprises here. Big outages at big firms seem to always look like this.The electricity industry has this notion they call a black start, where they bring the grid online from zero. It's a very difficult operation because you can't start a generator straight into an offline grid otherwise it gets hit with full demand and can't keep frequency in the right range, which can damage equipment. So they have to start by disconnecting all the loads and then bringing load blocks back in sequence, trying to roughly balance available generation with the size of the next block. In practice black starts are extremely rare, but they can happen after a cascading failure in which something causes some generating stations to trip, redirecting load onto the remaining stations which can't handle it causing them to trip in turn. The entire grid can collapse in just 60 seconds or so once that kind of runaway failure starts.This kind of outage is very analogous to a black start. A few servers get into trouble - maybe they're in a crash loop or maybe their caches are cold - the load balancers shift traffic onto the remainder which then can't handle the load either and so the failure cascades.
