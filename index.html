Nvidia allegedly engineered scarcity by funding AI startups to buy its GPUs

"Reading this thread makes me realize that seemingly no one has heard the basic, mundane case for why Nvidia would want to do so much "circular financing" as a matter of totally normal business strategy. This may not be the whole story, but it's worth understanding.Because of bottlenecks in the supply chain, Nvidia knows it is going to sell 100% of its inventory. They could raise prices quite a bit more, but then their only customers would be hyperscalers (building competing chips) and possibly OpenAI and Anthropic. Nvidia is willing to forgo present day profits for the strategic value of keeping its customer base diverse and promoting the network effects of e.g. CUDA. Instead of quoting a lower price per GPU to neoclouds and startups, they finance purchases of chips, but the two are roughly equivalent (financing means Nvidia takes on more risk, but has more upside and locks in future demand). It is in their commercial interest to have many competing buyers of their chips.Obviously this creates fun mechanisms for fraud and wacky accounting (and I'm agnostic as to whether that's happening here), but it's worth knowing about the boring/obvious story before donning your tinfoil hat."

"I see a few people saying they couldn’t get used to CGA, that it was absurd, or worse than no graphics at all.I can relate to that. But as I’ve aged I’ve appreciated just how much was done with less back. I mean that literally: we couldn’t afford a computer so my dad would haul home his work computer each weekend. We’d call BBS’ on it, print stuff. Play shareware games. It pissed my mom off because it took up half the dinner table. But my brother learned to code this way too.So later on when my friends got EGA and VGA, CGA meant “poor.”Fast forward many years and I have kids and were in better financial circumstances than my dad and mom back then. My kids have MacBooks. One of their friends was over one night and was visibly embarrassed that all she had was the school assigned Chromebook. She and my daughter were doing homework and her friend said that she wished her family could afford better laptops.I didn’t want to embarrass her but then next morning after I made pancakes and I was sitting at the table I was like hey let’s take a look at these Chromebooks (we had an old one sitting around) and the three of us sat there and in about an hour and a half they’d put together a very simple farm game in the spirit of Stardew Valley on PICO-8.Point being, we live in a world where especially with AI, it’s easy to think we can do everything. And it can feel like that, which means that when you can’t, you feel worse that you can’t because it would appear everyone else is doing amazing things with no limits.But maybe we need more limits on things. Maybe that 4 color palette forced devs and artists to be creative back then in ways that make things better for everyone."

"Every time I see CGA graphics in cyan, magenta, black and white it reminds me of my youth and how 'cold' it felt at the time to see those graphics, I could never really get used to it. When EGA/VGA graphics came along it felt so warm and in my experience games really started to come to life.Respect for the graphics artists in general for that time to be able to make something out of almost nothing, four colors or less and/or black and white low resolution."
