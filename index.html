Astra can read and write weights to improve Qwen

"Respectfully to the authors and to everyone here, I ask you to actually read the paper before losing your mind, or at the very least plug it into Claude. It is a cool project but not as nearly as insane as people say. I think the blog writing and subsequent posting overstate the results.Part of my research is in weight space learning. For starters, we have had the ability to edit weights, to generate new weights, to identify weights for a while. Here is an example for larger models as early as 2023: https://rome.baulab.info/Weight space interpretability and learning is an active research community, has been for the past 3 years with many great papers. I suspect it is hitting non-interp folks harder because the authors hyped up their work, and people who are not in the field did not bother to read it before typing up X posts.Some experiments here are done on very small models (20K params). We already know that small models are more interpretable. In fact, much of editing here you can do by hand. For Qwen, the model fixed a looping bug that may have been known apriori, and it is basically a steering vector, a very established technique. This is clear when they then attempt to "improve" math capability, and it doesn't work. For me, this paper rather shows that OpenAI has done training on mechinterp environment, in fact, maybe even on weight space learning datasets, which are open. But it does not show some insane "well past what we’d expect from humans or conventional algorithms" (from the blog post), more like past what I would expect from a large model with no specialized harness. I still think it is cool work, but do check out recent advances in autointerp, to ground your expectation.On a personal note, I understand the need for promotion of your work, but I think healthy skepticism is fine too. Otherwise, your understanding of what problems need to be worked on and solved in science becomes warped."

"This is insane.Astra can, without tooling, determine what games small networks are trained on, can edit the weights of networks directly to improve outcomes, and can hand edit a small qwen model to improve its math skills by like 5x.I'm shook. If you'd asked me this morning whether LLMs have native understanding of their weights that is less than human <-> neuron understanding, the same as human or more than human, I would have said "same to less" depending on definitions.No human can go edit a brain to become better at math. This is just very, very surprising to me and extremely interesting. I would guess this upsets quite a number of interp theories and philosophical takes on what's going on with these networks."

"And here we go...As others have said, while still absolutely insane, I don't find it completely out-of-the-blue surprising. One of the recurring abilities of (current-generation) LLMs seem to be to rapidly grasp very complex systems, where humans would need months or years to reach the same level of expertise.You can see this in the way a coding agent can "understand" and explain sprawling legacy codebases in seconds.You can also see it in the projects that are using them to reverse-engineer proprietary IoT protocols or computer games.But those were all systems where we had detailed knowledge about what structures are there to be reverse-enginered.If the models can transfer this ability to some other systems where we don't know the underlying structure then I think we will be in for a lot more craziness in the future. (Protein interaction networks? DNA sequences? Ecosystem interactions? Animal languages?)As for weights, if the models really can analyze the semantics behind specific weights or directions, I have my hopes that they could also give a textual explanation or help designing tools to make "handcrafting" weights more practical in general."

"Besides a rewrite to another framework using AI, another option is to modernize the framework with AI instead. Personally, I started to like Mithril after having worked with it for a few years. Instead of rewriting it to a better maintained alternative (mithril doesn't have much active development going on since 2015), I used agents to add signals, proper state management, typing, ssr, computed properties, Bun tooling and more to it. "This benchmark is too simplified to be meaningful. Mathematics has changed. Let's act like it

I would give that person substantial credit even if they supplied no new ingredient in the original proof.Yup. This is what Terence Tao has been saying about digestion of results for several months now. Mathematics has become a field where results themselves matter less, the social aspect of attracting others to understand your results and explore new problems has become more important.In a sense it is a little bit like software engineering, where writing code can sometimes be delegated to AI, and is arguably less important than understanding what the customer really wants from this software."

"I really struggle to understand this discussion. Mathematics isn't a way to keep a specific kind of intellectual happy by providing fun, meaningless puzzles to solve in isolation. Supermarket freezers across Europe hit by second suspected cyber-attack

"As I mentioned in the thread regarding the previous attacks, I worked in a closely related field back in 2008-2009.One thing I see repeatedly brought up here in this discussion is "Why can't we just set the refrigerator temperature with a knob and walk away?"The problem is that these refrigerator cases are not as simple as your refrigerator at home. As a simple example, many systems allow you have control over the deadband: the exact low temperature at which the compressor turns off and the high temperature at which the compressor turns back on. You also have control over defrost cycles, compressor pressure and many other things (I worked with this a long while ago, and I don't remember all the details).Take a look at some of these manuals, for example: https://www.hillphoenix.com/resource/installation-manuals-2/... As far as I can tell, those manuals just cover the refrigerator cases, and not the outside compressors and heat exchangers, which are another layer to deal with.This is not something that a non-expert supermarket employee would be able to handle well. The purpose of all these settings is optimize around energy usage, maintenance costs, customer experience, etc. Even if you only save $500/store/year, you can see that it really adds up over a chain of 1000 stores.That's why this equipment is remotely accessible: it's not like your fridge at home and requires a lot more knowledge to get the most out of it. Of course, that does not excuse poor security implementations."

"Bless those hackers for firing a warning shot over our freezers, that we may take heed and secure our neonatal care units, stoplights, and air traffic controllers."

"I'm extrapolating a bit here based on Reddit hearsay, but I think this may have to do with rural stores having essentially consumer-grade internet routers connected to the mobile broadband network.Much of the coverage in Swedish media drives the point that only 4G-uplinked stores have been hit so far. I think that's unfortunate, because really it wouldn't have differed if these stores were connected by 5G.I'm guessing here, but my money would be on freezer ICS systems being made available to the world by accident by way of NAT-PMP on a consumer router. In Scandinavia you can bypass CG-NAT on mobile connections by setting a particular APN, so my guess is that these control systems were simply findable on Shodan.This also explains why this wasn't discovered after the recent French breach. Supposedly all the stores with "proper" uplinks were in compliance, and no one had thought of a way in for those non-compliant stores, because they "weren't even connected, so how would an attacker get in?""
